For decades, Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) has been a discipline built on spreadsheets, checklists, and after-the-fact audits. Teams reacted to regulatory change, chased down evidence for controls, and manually stitched together risk pictures from disconnected systems.

## Why GRC Needed to Change

Three pressures are converging on compliance and risk functions at once:

- **Regulatory sprawl.** New rules on data privacy, AI governance, ESG disclosure, and cybersecurity are arriving faster than most teams can track them.
- **Data volume.** Modern enterprises generate far more transactional, behavioral, and system data than any human team can review.
- **Third-party risk.** Organizations now depend on sprawling vendor and partner ecosystems.

## Where AI Is Actually Being Used

### Continuous Control Monitoring
Instead of quarterly manual testing, machine learning models can watch control performance in near real time.

### Regulatory Change Management
Natural language processing can scan new regulations and map relevant clauses to existing control frameworks.

> The organizations that treat AI as a shift in how GRC work gets done — rather than a shortcut around doing it — are the ones that will end up with both stronger compliance postures and less burned-out compliance teams.
